# Stock Market Dark Style Bible

## Mood
Cold, premium, and psychologically tense. The world should feel expensive but unsafe, like wealth is always visible and never fully yours.

## Palette
- abyss black: `#04070b`
- carbon blue: `#0b1621`
- ticker green: `#79d8a6`
- panic red: `#c76c6c`
- aged gold: `#d3b174`
- paper gray: `#c7c2bb`

## Lighting
- hard top light for institutional and manipulation scenes
- laptop and phone glow for retail anxiety scenes
- low ambient fill with selective highlights for crash sequences
- soft dawn lift only in the realistic-profitability ending

## Texture system
- subtle film grain across the full documentary
- faint bloom on green and red market elements
- matte paper texture for evidence and rule-card scenes
- occasional CRT softness for ticker-wall and media scenes

## Motion grammar
- slow push-ins for dread and consequence
- sharper lateral motion for hype, media, and narrative manipulation
- restrained parallax on stills rather than constant aggressive camera movement
- pattern interrupt every 20 to 30 seconds through device change, scale change, or negative space

## Typography rules
- serif headlines for philosophy and emotional consequence
- clean sans-serif for labels, data, and system explanations
- one short phrase at a time for kinetic scenes
- isolate key words like `fear`, `leverage`, `late`, `discipline`, and `survive`

## Device routing
- `hero-panel`: slow premium still with thesis framing
- `proof-grid`: evidence-like stacking and contradiction framing
- `kinetic-quotes`: pressure words, social-media slogans, FOMO language
- `network-map`: invisible system and institutional advantage
- `crash-timeline`: historical panic beats
- `split-contrast`: retail vs institutions, investing vs gambling, patience vs urgency
- `leverage-cliff`: visual metaphor for borrowed-risk collapse
- `bubble-curve`: repeated cycle pattern
- `rule-cards`: realistic survival and profitability framework

## Texture pass notes
- never overdo glitch
- no fake CNBC noise collage unless it directly serves a line
- keep the world tactile and believable rather than stylized for its own sake
