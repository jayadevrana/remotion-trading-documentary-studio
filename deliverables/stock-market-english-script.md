# Stock Market Documentary Script

## Core angle
- controlling question: Who really wins when ordinary people enter the stock market?
- thesis: The stock market can build wealth, but only if you understand that it is not just a machine of numbers. It is a machine of incentives, pressure, emotion, manipulation, and patience.
- surface story: A dark, gripping explanation of how the stock market works and why it feels so brutal to ordinary investors.
- deeper story: Modern investing has become a psychological survival system where fear of falling behind pushes people into a game they barely understand.
- why now: Inflation, app-based investing, online hype, and instant financial content have made market participation feel urgent rather than optional.
- ending payoff: Real profitability exists, but it is painfully unglamorous. The market rewards discipline far more often than excitement.

## Title package
- titles:
  - The Dark Truth About The Stock Market
  - Who Really Wins In The Stock Market?
  - Why Ordinary Investors Lose
  - The Market Sells Hope
  - The Stock Market Is Not What You Think
- thumbnail concepts:
  - a lone investor facing a giant glowing ticker wall
  - a green candlestick turning into a cliff edge
  - a split between euphoric hype and quiet financial collapse
- best pairing and why: `Who Really Wins In The Stock Market?` with the candlestick cliff. It immediately creates tension, danger, and class imbalance.

## Retention map
- cold open promise: The market is sold as safety, but for millions it feels more like a machine that punishes hesitation and feeds on emotion.
- open loops:
  - If investing is supposed to help ordinary people, why do so many lose when they finally enter?
  - Why do crashes keep repeating even when everyone knows they happen?
  - Is there any realistic path to profitability that is not fantasy?
- act 1 turn: The promise of freedom becomes a system of pressure.
- act 2 turn: The mechanics give way to the emotional engine and the institutional edge.
- act 3 payoff: The film answers the profitability question honestly and ends with a cold but useful mental model.
- callback line: The market offers opportunity, but never mercy.

## Evidence ledger
### Verified
- Stocks represent ownership claims in companies.
- Major panic episodes referenced include 1929, 1987, the dot-com collapse, the 2008 financial crisis, the March 2020 pandemic crash, and the 2021 meme-stock frenzy.
- Leverage magnifies both gains and losses.

### Attributed
- The claim that institutions usually operate with better tools, deeper research, faster information flow, and more room to survive mistakes is structural rather than absolute.
- The comparison between day trading and gambling is behavioral framing, not a legal classification.

### Inferred
- The documentary’s philosophical framing around dependency, emotional pressure, and social comparison is interpretive rather than statistical.
